Icons in row labels, and a histogram. Without the new props and data fields, every component renders as in 1.5.0, byte for byte.

- **Icons in row labels.** `DataTable` rows and `RankingVarianceChart` data take an `icon` - a `RowIcon` (`src`, `naturalSize`, and an optional `pixelRatio`, `border` colour and `alt`) or several, drawn left to right as chips - and a `labelColor` for the label. An icon is drawn at the component's new `iconSize` (default 20; in `DataTable` from 8 to 28, in the ranking chart from 8 to its row height minus 4), but never above `naturalSize / pixelRatio` CSS px - the size at which its file stays crisp on a screen of that pixel ratio (default 1) - nor above 56 CSS px. A `border` is a 1 px frame drawn over the icon's edge, so the image keeps its full size. An icon is decorative unless it has an `alt`, which is read before the label, set apart by a comma. Rows without an icon, and the totals row, keep the slot's width, so the labels stay aligned. The ranking chart's label column grows by the slot, up to 40% of its width, taking the room from the AC panel first (down to half of it), so the variance panels keep their widths; chips that would cut a label under 24 px are left out from the end, counted by a "+N" (the new word `moreIcons`), and named in the label's tooltip. `renderIcon(row, size, icon)` puts your own element in the slot instead - HTML in `DataTable`, svg in the ranking chart - and hands you the row's icons as the component would draw them, to wrap in a tooltip trigger without redrawing them.
- **`HistogramChart`.** A distribution in classes of identical width (IBCS CH 1.3) on a horizontal value axis. Pass raw `values`, binned by the new `computeHistogram` in `ibcs-react/core` - the Freedman-Diaconis rule bounded to 20-60 classes (`binning`), counts summing to n, edges covering the smallest to the largest value - or an engine's own `bins` (a start, one width, the counts). The mean (`mean`) is a line from above the columns down to the top of the column it falls in, with a tick under the axis, so it never splits a class. Its interval - a half-width and the level it was computed at, both yours; the chart never derives one - is drawn as an error bar and written "Mean 910.8 ± 0.9 (95%)": an interval comes with its level or not at all, and a mean alone is written without "±". The spread of the values (`spread`; by default the middle half, from the first to the third quartile, captioned by its own `label` when you give one) is a band behind the columns, bracketed and captioned under the axis - two marks, two rows, two names, so they are never confused. Labels replace grid lines: every count above its column when all fit, else the tallest's; the edges at the two ends of the axis, the last left out when both do not fit, and either left out when the mean's tick falls on it; n on the title line. The columns wear their `scenario`'s IBCS notation. Numbers follow `format` (exact `fractionDigits`, `locale`), but the class edges, the spread and the mean are written in full unless `format` sets `compact: true`: at `fractionDigits`, or without them to one decimal place past the class width's first significant digit, so neighbouring edges never read alike. With `compact: true` they are compact, with the digits added that keep neighbouring edges apart. A screen-reader table lists every class with its count and share.
- **New words** in `IbcsLabels`: `count` ("Count"), `mean` ("Mean {value}"), `spread` ("Middle half {range}"), `sampleSize` ("n = {n}") and `moreIcons` ("+{count}"). They are optional in the `IbcsLabels` type, so a complete 1.5.0 dictionary still compiles, and the defaults fill the ones it lacks. `defaultLabels`, `mergeLabels` and `useIbcsLabels` keep their 1.5.0 type, `IbcsLabels`, and their values hold every word. The new `IbcsLabelsComplete` type requires every word, so a translator can type a dictionary with it to be told of the words to translate.
