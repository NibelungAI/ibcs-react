A timeline, range bars, point callouts and fans. Without the new components and props, every component renders as in 1.6.0, byte for byte - except `LineChart`'s thinned period labels, which a dense series no longer cuts to "…" (below).

- **`TimelineChart`.** A Gantt chart of one run: time across (IBCS UN 3.3), a row per ability, buff or task (UN 3.4). A row's `ticks` are moments - casts - drawn as ticks over a thin halo; its `spans` are intervals: an `"active"` span (a buff up, a channel) is a bar, a `"waiting"` span (a cooldown) a thin light line. `annotations` are numbered notes: each number sits above the rows with an assisting line down to its moment - to just above its `row`'s marks, or across every row - and its text is written beside the same number under the chart (UN 2.4); numbers too close to sit at their moments are set side by side, and a long note wraps, never cut. The time axis is labelled at nice steps, as many as their labels leave room for, without grid lines; `timeFormat="clock"` writes seconds as minutes and seconds ("1:30", "1:02:03"), the default writes numbers by `format`. `domain` fixes the range: a span outside it is clipped and a tick left out, both still in the screen-reader table. Rows take icons before their labels, as `DataTable`'s, with `iconSize` and `renderIcon`. The chart derives nothing: the caller turns a log into rows. The new `computeTimeline` and `formatClock` are in `ibcs-react/core`.
- **`RangeBarChart`.** Floating range bars after J.P. Morgan's _Guide to the Markets_: each category's two ends (`from`, `to` - the 10th and 90th percentile, or an estimate ± its half-width), its `mark` as a hollow dot on the bar (never a cut that splits it), and both ends labelled outside the bar and the dot. `orientation="bars"` (the default) gives each category a row, with icons and quality-coloured names in the labels; `orientation="columns"` gives each a column, for a sequence - an estimate narrowing wave by wave - and moves an end label that would touch another label, a column or a dot away from its end, with a leader. The value axis spans the data, and the bars wear their `scenario`'s IBCS notation. Values are written in full unless `compact: true`: at `fractionDigits`, or without them at one decimal past the narrowest range's first significant digit, one count for the chart, so no range's ends read alike. `computeRangeBars` is in `ibcs-react/core`.
- **Point callouts.** `LineChart` and `TrendChart` take `callouts`: labelled points the caller names (`{ index, label, series?, placement? }`, "\n" for a new line), as on GTM p. 4. Each label is placed clear of every line, marker, label, reference caption and other callout - right beside its point (above a peak, below a trough; above a column's value label, below a negative column), else moved away with a leader; a label beside a column or a band's edge is tied to it by a short line, and the plot keeps room for the labels. A callout that finds no room is numbered at its point and written under the chart, which grows by that list; when even its number has no room, its note names its period. None is left out. The screen-reader table carries each callout's text in its period's row.
- **Fans.** `LineChart` takes `fans`: bands whose edges vary from period to period (`{ from, to, label?, color? }`, a value per period), drawn faint beneath the lines and broken at gaps - the 10th to the 90th percentile at every moment of a fight, say. The value axis grows to hold them; a band's `label` is placed beside its right end, tied to its edge, and names its column of the screen-reader table.
- **New words** in `IbcsLabels`: `events` ("Events"), `event` ("Event"), `active` ("Active"), `waiting` ("Waiting"), `rangeLow` ("Low"), `rangeHigh` ("High"), `rangeMark` ("Mean"), `note` ("Note") and `band` ("Band {n}"). They are optional in `IbcsLabels`, so a complete 1.5.0 or 1.6.0 dictionary still compiles, and the defaults fill the ones it lacks. `IbcsLabelsComplete`, the strict type for a full translation, now requires them too, as it is meant to: a dictionary typed with it is told of the nine words to translate.
- **Fix: `LineChart`'s thinned period labels.** A series with more than 12 periods writes every n-th period's label; each was cut to the width of one period, so a dense series showed only "…". Each now takes the room of the periods it stands for, never past the chart's left edge.
