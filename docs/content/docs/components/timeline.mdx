import { TimelineChart } from "ibcs-react";
import { fightNotes, fightRows } from "@/lib/demo-data/marks";

A timeline lays one run out in time: time runs horizontally (IBCS UN 3.3)
and each ability, buff or task has a row of its own (UN 3.4). It answers
"what happened when?": the casts of one simulated fight, the steps of a
project, the stages of a batch job.

## When to use

When the order and overlap of events matter - which buffs were up during a
burst, how often an ability waited on its cooldown. For how a value evolved,
use a line chart; for how often something happened across many runs, a table
or a histogram.

```tsx
    {
      label: "Aimed Shot",

    },
    { label: "Rapid Fire", ticks: [30], spans: [{ from: 30, to: 45 }] }, // a buff's uptime
  ]}
  annotations={[{ at: 30, row: 2, text: "Rapid Fire: 15 s of 40% faster shots" }]}
/>;
```

## Ticks, bars and lines

Each row draws what it is given:

- **`ticks`** are moments - the casts of an ability - drawn as short vertical
  lines in ink, taller than a bar, so a cast stands out of the bar it falls on
  and the bar stays whole: a buff refreshed while it is up reads as one bar.
- **`spans`** are intervals. An `"active"` span (the default) - a buff up, a
  cast or a channel - is a bar; a `"waiting"` span - a cooldown - is a thin
  light line. The two never look alike.

The chart derives nothing. Turning a combat log into rows - which cast is a
tick, how long a cooldown runs - is the caller's work, and so is choosing the
run to show: label it as one example, as the title above does. Marks outside
`domain` are clipped (a span) or left out (a tick); the screen-reader table
still lists them.

## Numbered annotations

An annotation names a moment and, optionally, the row whose mark it explains.
Its number sits in a disc above the rows, with an assisting line down to the
moment - to just above that row's marks, or across every row to the axis -
and its text is written beside the same number under the chart (IBCS UN 2.4:
annotations integrated in the chart and linked by references). Numbers too
close to sit at their moments are set side by side, each line still at its
exact moment. A long note wraps between words; none is cut. An annotation
outside `domain` keeps its number, at the nearest end, without a line.

## The time axis

The axis is labelled at nice steps - 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, or on a
clock (`timeFormat="clock"`) whole seconds, minutes and hours - as many as
their labels leave room for: no two labels ever touch, and none leaves the
chart. There is no grid line. Times in the tooltip and the table take
`format.fractionDigits` (on a clock, one digit by default: "1:05.5"); without
a clock, `format.suffix` carries the unit (`{ suffix: " s" }`) and values are
written in full unless `compact` is `true`. Compact times take the fewest more
digits that tell two neighbours apart: an axis from 1,000 to 1,100 s reads
"1.00K" to "1.10K", never "1K" six times, and so do the tooltip and the table.

Write the same times outside the chart with `formatClock` (from
`ibcs-react/core`): `formatClock(65.5, { fractionDigits: 1 })` is "1:05.5".

## Icons in the row labels

A row's `icon` is drawn before its label, as in `DataTable`: at `iconSize`
(default 16), never above `naturalSize / pixelRatio` CSS px, every label
aligned after the widest row's icons. `renderIcon` wraps or replaces a row's
icons - in a tooltip trigger of your own, say.

## Words and accessibility

The svg is one labelled image: its title, then "Timeline of 6 rows, 0:00.0
to 3:00.0." for the example above (`ariaLabel` replaces both). Beside it, a visually hidden table lists
every row's moments and intervals, with the notes in its caption. Its
columns, and the tooltip on a mark, take the chart's words: `events`
("Events"), `event` ("Event"), `active` ("Active") and `waiting` ("Waiting").
