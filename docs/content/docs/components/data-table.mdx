The general comparison table: rows are entities (regions, teams, products),
columns are measures. A column is a plain value, a variance drawn as an
embedded bar or pin, or a sparkline - so magnitude, deviation and trend sit
side by side in one grid.

## When to use

A cross-entity comparison: revenue by region, headcount by team, cost by
category. Use [StatementTable](/docs/components/statement-table) instead when
rows build up to subtotals (a P&L or a balance sheet), and
[ComparisonTable](/docs/components/comparison-table) when the row labels should
sit in the centre, flanked by two period groups.

## Example

Six regions and two measures (revenue and operating income), each with an
absolute ΔPY bar; revenue also gets a relative-variance pin and a sparkline.
Header clicks re-sort, and `showTotals` appends a summed row.

    showTotals

</Frame>

Rows may nest through `children`: a parent renders as a bold group that sums
its children and collapses on click.

## Magnitude bars

A value column with `mark: "bar"` draws the value itself as a bar - an IBCS
integrated bar chart, whose length is the size, not a difference to a base -
with its number beside it; `mark: "pin"` draws a dot on a stem instead. The
detail (leaf) rows share one scale that always contains zero (negative values
grow left of a shifted axis), and the bar wears its scenario's notation (AC
solid, PY grey, PL frame, FC hatch). The totals row and group rows keep the
plain number: a sum is off the detail rows' scale, and a bar cut at its edge
would draw 160 as long as 100. The bars stop 8px short of the column's widest
number, so no number overlaps a bar or a whisker; the column grows past its
160px when its numbers need the room.

`fractionDigits` keeps every cell of a column at the same digits: Raptor
Strike's share of 9.996 % reads "10.0", not "10". A column's `format` replaces
the table's `format` for that column - it is not merged - so repeat the
table's `locale`, `compact` and the rest in it. See
[Numbers and words](/docs/localization).

## Intervals and bands

`row.errors` holds the half-width of an interval - a 95% confidence interval,
say - per column key (a value and its variance each have their own). A plain
value column writes "914.30 ± 0.47"; a bar or pin column - a value column with
a `mark`, or a variance column - draws an error bar on its mark, which carries
the interval as its accessible text and tooltip. An off-scale Δ% arrow has no
whisker, so the interval is the arrow's tooltip. A pin column with errors moves
its labels into a right-hand gutter so no whisker meets a label. Half-widths
are never summed into parents or the totals row.

A positive half-width is never written as zero (within Intl's 20 fraction
digits). With `fractionDigits` it keeps
the column's digits and takes more when its first significant figure needs
them ("914.30 ± 0.004"); without, both numbers are written in full at the
digits two significant figures of the half-width need, so the default format
writes 914.3013 ± 0.4682 as "914.30 ± 0.47", not "914 ± 0".

`column.bands` shades value ranges across the body rows of a bar or pin column,
in the units its mark is drawn in - here the variants tied with the best. Which
rows are tied is your rule; the band only draws it. A band's `label` is its
tooltip, and the table's screen-reader caption names it after your `caption`:
"Δ vs equipped, Tied with the best: +3.00–+3.92".

On an abs variance column drawn as a `bar` the axis is the bar's length |Δ|
from 0, so its band is given as lengths and a whisker that reaches past zero is
cut at the axis (an open end). `ComparisonTable` reads the same column type but
draws marks, error bars and bands in `DataTable` only.

## Icons in row labels

A row's `icon` puts a small picture before its label - an item or spell icon,
a portrait - or several, left to right, as chips. Each is drawn at the table's
`iconSize` (default 20, at most 28 in its 34 px rows), but never above the
image's own `naturalSize`: an 18 px file stays 18 px, so an icon is never
upscaled. Its `border` frames it in a colour of your choosing, inside its box -
an item's quality colour. A row's `labelColor` colours its label.

<Frame>

- **The label stays the row's name.** An icon is decorative by default - its
  `alt` is "" - so a screen reader reads the label alone. Give an `alt` when
  the picture says what the label does not (the items a variant changes); it is
  read before the label, as the markup orders them.
- **Labels stay aligned.** Every row keeps the room of the widest row's icons,
  a row without an icon and the totals row too.
- **Your own element.** `renderIcon(row, size)` returns an element for a row's
  slot in place of its `icon` - an icon that opens your own tooltip, a spell
  icon with its school marker. Return null to fall back to the row's `icon`.
  Its size and its accessible text are yours.
- **Contrast.** A quality colour on a label is text: keep it at 4.5:1 against
  the table's surface. Epic `#a335ee` and rare `#0070dd` do on white; the
  uncommon green `#1eff00` does not.

## Sorting: controlled or uncontrolled

Clicking a header cycles the sort: descending, then ascending, then off.

- **Uncontrolled** - seed the initial order with `defaultSort` (`{ key, dir }`,
  or `null` for unsorted) and let the table own it. `onSortChange` still fires
  as an observer.
- **Controlled** - pass `sort` (or `null`) plus `onSortChange`. The table
  renders exactly that sort and never mutates it; each header activation
  reports the next sort - `null` when the cycle clears it - for you to apply.

The collapsed groups follow the same convention: `defaultCollapsed` is the
uncontrolled seed, `collapsed` (a `ReadonlySet<string>` or string array) plus
`onCollapsedChange` is the controlled pair. Once `collapsed` is provided, the
matching `default*` prop is ignored.

```tsx
const [sort, setSort] = useState<DataTableSort | null>({ key: "rev", dir: "desc" });
const [collapsed, setCollapsed] = useState<string[]>([]);

<DataTable
  columns={columns}
  rows={rows}
  sort={sort}
  onSortChange={setSort}
  collapsed={collapsed}
  onCollapsedChange={setCollapsed}
/>;
```
